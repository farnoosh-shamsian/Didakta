# §Voc. Exclamations and direct address[^48]

The vocative is used in exclamations and in direct address: 

→ “[**ὦ**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BD%A6%2A%2A) **[Ζεῦ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%96%CE%B5%E1%BF%A6)=)** [καὶ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BA%CE%B1%E1%BD%B6) [**θεοί**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%CE%B8%CE%B5%CE%BF%CE%AF%2A%2A)” [**P. Pr. 310d**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Plat.%20Prot.%20310d&lang=original); ⇾ oh Zeus and ye gods

→ “[**ἄνθρωπε**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BC%84%CE%BD%CE%B8%CF%81%CF%89%CF%80%CE%B5%2A%2A)” [**X. C. 2.2.7**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Xen.%20Cyrop.%202.2.7&lang=original); ⇾ my good fellow 

In ordinary conversation and public speeches, the polite [ὦ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%A6) is usually added. Without [ὦ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%A6)=) the vocative may express astonishment, joy, contempt, a threat, or a warning, etc.:

→ “[ἀκούεις](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%80%CE%BA%CE%BF%CF%8D%CE%B5%CE%B9%CF%82)=) [**Αἰσχίνη**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%CE%91%E1%BC%B0%CF%83%CF%87%CE%AF%CE%BD%CE%B7%2A%2A)kou/eis);” [**D. 18.121**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Dem.%2018.121&lang=original); ⇾ *d'ye hear*, Aeschines? 

But this distinction is not always observed, though in general [ὦ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%A6)sxi/nh) has a familiar tone which was unsuited to elevated poetry.

# §NomInd. Independent[^3]

The nominative may be used independently in citing the names of persons and things.

→ “[προσείληφε](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%80%CF%81%CE%BF%CF%83%CE%B5%CE%AF%CE%BB%CE%B7%CF%86%CE%B5)=n) [τὴν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%84%E1%BD%B4%CE%BD) [τῶν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%84%E1%BF%B6%CE%BD) [πονηρῶν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%80%CE%BF%CE%BD%CE%B7%CF%81%E1%BF%B6%CE%BD) [κοινὴν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BA%CE%BF%CE%B9%CE%BD%E1%BD%B4%CE%BD) [ἐπωνυμίαν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%90%CF%80%CF%89%CE%BD%CF%85%CE%BC%CE%AF%CE%B1%CE%BD) [**συκοφάντης**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%CF%83%CF%85%CE%BA%CE%BF%CF%86%CE%AC%CE%BD%CF%84%CE%B7%CF%82%2A%2A)pwnumi/an)” [**Aeschin. 2.99**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Aeschin.%202.99&lang=original); ⇾ he received the common appellation of the vile, i.e. ‘informer’

→ “[τὸ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%84%E1%BD%B8) [δ᾽](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%B4%E1%BE%BD) [**ὑμεῖς**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BD%91%CE%BC%CE%B5%E1%BF%96%CF%82%2A%2A) [ὅταν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%85%CF%84%CE%B1%CE%BD) [λέγω](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BB%CE%AD%CE%B3%CF%89), [λέγω](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BB%CE%AD%CE%B3%CF%89) [τὴν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%84%E1%BD%B4%CE%BD) [πόλιν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%80%CF%8C%CE%BB%CE%B9%CE%BD)” [**D. 18.88**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Dem.%2018.88&lang=original); ⇾ when I say You, I mean the State

Independent nominatives are also used in incomplete sentences:

Soph. 266d**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Plat.%20Soph.%20266d&lang=original); ⇾ I assume two kinds of poetry: the divine and the human

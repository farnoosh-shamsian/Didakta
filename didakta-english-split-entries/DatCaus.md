# §DatCaus. Cause or Purpose[^31]

For the dative of purpose (to what end?), a predicate noun is used: 

“[ἐκείνῳ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%90%CE%BA%CE%B5%CE%AF%CE%BD%E1%BF%B3) [ἡ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%A1)kei/nw|) [χώρα](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%87%CF%8E%CF%81%CE%B1) [**δῶρον**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%CE%B4%E1%BF%B6%CF%81%CE%BF%CE%BD%2A%2A) [ἐδόθη](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%90%CE%B4%CF%8C%CE%B8%CE%B7)” [**X. H. 3.1.6**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Xen.%20Hell.%203.1.6&lang=original); ⇾ the country was given to him as a gift

The dative, especially with verbs of emotion, expresses the occasion (external cause) or the motive (internal cause). Sometimes the dative of cause may resemble the dative of purpose.

# §MidIndir. Indirect Reflexive Middle[^86]

In the indirect reflexive middle, the subject of the verb is affected by the verb, often as a beneficiary. The Indirect Reflexive Middle represents the subject as acting *for himself, with reference to himself*, or *with something belonging to himself*. *Self* is often the indirect object. So πορίζεσθαι provide for oneself (πορίζειν provide), φυλάττεσθαι guard against (φυλάττειν keep guard), αἱρεῖσθαι choose (take for oneself). The middle often denotes that the subject acts with something belonging to himself (material objects, means, powers). It is often used for acts done willingly. 

→ “ὅπλα **πορίσασθαι**” **T. 4.9**; ⇾ to procure arms for themselves

→ “ὁπλίτας **μετεπέμψατο**” **T. 7\. 31**; ⇾ he sent for hoplites

→ “γυναῖκα **ἠγαγόμην**” **L. 1.6**; ⇾ I married

Some middle verbs may indicate that the subject has something done by another for himself. This use is often called the causative middle.

→ “ἐγὼ γάρ σε ταῦτα **ἐδιδαξάμην**” **X. C. 1.6.2**; ⇾ for I had you taught this

→ “**παρατίθεσθαι** σῖτον” **X. C. 8\. 6\. 12**; ⇾ to have food served up

→ “ὅσοι ὅπλα ἀφῄρηνται, ταχὺ ἄλλα **ποιήσονται**” **X. C. 6\. 1\. 12**; ⇾ all who have had their arms taken from them will soon get others made

→ “ἑαυτῷ σκηνὴν **κατεσκευάσατο**” **X. C. 2\. 1\. 30**; ⇾ he had a tent prepared for himself

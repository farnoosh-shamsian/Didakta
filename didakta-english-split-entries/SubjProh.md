# §SubjProh. Prohibitive Subjunctive[^99]

The subjunctive (in the second and third persons of the aorist) is often used to express prohibitions (negative [μή](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%CE%AE)). The third person is less common and usually represents the second person (see example below).

→ “[μηδὲν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%CE%B7%CE%B4%E1%BD%B2%CE%BD) [**ἀθυμήσητε**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BC%80%CE%B8%CF%85%CE%BC%CE%AE%CF%83%CE%B7%CF%84%CE%B5%2A%2A)” [**X. A. 5.4.19**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Xen.%20Anab.%205.4.19&lang=original); ⇾ do not lose heart

→ “[**ὑπολάβῃ**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BD%91%CF%80%CE%BF%CE%BB%CE%AC%CE%B2%E1%BF%83%2A%2A) [δὲ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%B4%E1%BD%B2) [μηδείς](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%CE%B7%CE%B4%CE%B5%CE%AF%CF%82)” [**T. 6.84**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Thuc.%206.84&lang=original); ⇾ and let no one suppose ( \= [μὴ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%E1%BD%B4) [ὑπολάβητε](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%91%CF%80%CE%BF%CE%BB%CE%AC%CE%B2%CE%B7%CF%84%CE%B5) do not suppose).

[οὐ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BF%E1%BD%90)w/meqa) [μή](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%CE%AE)) with the subjunctive of the second person in the dramatic poets occasionally expresses a strong prohibition: 

→ “[οὐ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BF%E1%BD%90) [μὴ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%E1%BD%B4)) [**ληρήσῃς**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%CE%BB%CE%B7%CF%81%CE%AE%CF%83%E1%BF%83%CF%82%2A%2A)” [**Ar. Nub. 367**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Aristoph.%20Cl.%20367&lang=original); ⇾ don't talk nonsense

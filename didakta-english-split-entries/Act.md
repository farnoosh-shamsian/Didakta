# §Act. Active Voice[^85]

The active voice represents the subject as performing the action of the verb: [λούω](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BB%CE%BF%CF%8D%CF%89) I wash. Under *action* is included *being*, as [ἡ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%A1) [ὁδὸς](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%81%CE%B4%E1%BD%B8%CF%82) [μακρά](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%CE%B1%CE%BA%CF%81%CE%AC) [ἐστι](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%90%CF%83%CF%84%CE%B9) the way is long. 

Active verbs may be *transitive* or *intransitive.* Verbs capable of taking a direct object are called transitive and other verbs are intransitive. Many verbs are used in the active voice both transitively and intransitively. The distinction between transitive and intransitive verbs is a grammatical convenience, and is not founded on an essential difference of nature.

# §AorIter. Customary Action (Iterative)[^72]

The aorist with [ἄν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%84%CE%BD)fqalmou/s) may indicate repetitions and habits. Additionally, Homer and Herodotus have iterative imperfects and aorists in \-[σκον](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%83%CE%BA%CE%BF%CE%BD) and \-[σκομην](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%83%CE%BA%CE%BF%CE%BC%CE%B7%CE%BD) denoting a customary or repeated past action. Homer has iterative forms in the imperfect and 1 and 2 aorist active and middle. Herodotus has no iteratives in the 1 aorist and few in the 2 aorist.

→ “[**εἶπεν**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%CE%B5%E1%BC%B6%CF%80%CE%B5%CE%BD%2A%2A)/n) [ἄν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%84%CE%BD)=pen)” [**X. C. 7.1.14**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Xen.%20Cyrop.%207.1.14&lang=original); ⇾ he used to say 

→ “[ἂν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%82%CE%BD)/n) [**ἔλεξεν**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BC%94%CE%BB%CE%B5%CE%BE%CE%B5%CE%BD%2A%2A)\\n)” [**X. C. 7.1.10**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Xen.%20Cyrop.%207.1.10&lang=original); ⇾ he was wont to say

# §ArtGener. Generic[^124]

The generic article denotes an entire class as distinguished from other classes. Thus, [ὁ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BD%81) [ἄνθρωπος](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%E1%BC%84%CE%BD%CE%B8%CF%81%CF%89%CF%80%CE%BF%CF%82) man (as distinguished from other beings), [οἱ](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BF%E1%BC%B1)/nqrwpos) [γέροντες](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%B3%CE%AD%CF%81%CE%BF%CE%BD%CF%84%CE%B5%CF%82) the aged.

→ “[πονηρὸν](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%80%CE%BF%CE%BD%CE%B7%CF%81%E1%BD%B8%CE%BD) [**ὁ**](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%2A%2A%E1%BD%81%2A%2A) [συκοφάντης](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CF%83%CF%85%CE%BA%CE%BF%CF%86%CE%AC%CE%BD%CF%84%CE%B7%CF%82)” [**D. 18.242**](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Dem.%2018.242&lang=original); ⇾ the informer is a vile thing

In the singular the generic article makes a single object the representative of the entire class; in the plural it denotes all the objects belonging to a class. The generic article is especially common, in the plural, with adjectives used substantively. A participle with the article may also denote an entire class.
